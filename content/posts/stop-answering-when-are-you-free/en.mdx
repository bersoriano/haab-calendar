It's 9:40 at night and your phone buzzes. "Hi! Do you have anything Thursday?"

You open your calendar. Thursday at 3 is free, so you offer it. An hour later: "Can't do 3. Anything in the morning?" By then someone else has asked about Thursday morning, and you're trying to remember whether you promised them 10:30 or only mentioned it.

Nobody did anything wrong. This is simply what booking looks like when you are the booking system.

Some businesses try to escape by adding an online booking page, and run into a different problem. A client finds the page, picks a time, and gets asked to create an account and choose a password, just to see the dentist. Some will do it. Others close the tab, and you never find out they were there.

Haab Calendar is built around a short promise: **one link, no sign-up, no double bookings.** Here's why each part of that matters.

## What the current way really costs

The price of manual booking rarely shows up on an invoice. It shows up in three other places.

**Your time.** A single appointment can take four or five messages to settle: the question, the offer, the counteroffer, the confirmation, the "sorry, can we move it?" Each one is short. Together they pull you away from the work people actually pay you for, and they follow you home.

**Your clients' patience.** People usually book when they have a spare minute, and that minute is often not during your office hours. If the answer has to wait until you're free, they may ask someone else in the meantime. A sign-up form does similar damage in a different way. Asking for a password from someone who only wants a Tuesday appointment is one more reason to give up.

**Your reputation.** Two people believe they have the same slot. Maybe you replied to both from memory. Maybe two clients filled out the same form within a minute of each other. However it happened, one of them is about to get an apologetic message from you, and neither will remember it as a small mistake.

## The simple alternative: one link

Your page gets one link. You put it wherever people already try to reach you, and from then on it answers the question you've been answering by hand.

Here's what happens when a client opens it.

**They see what's actually open.** Availability is live, so nobody picks a time you can't honor, and nobody has to ask first.

**They don't create anything.** No account, no password, no app. They choose a time, enter their details, and they're done. There's also no client password for you to store or for them to forget.

**Their slot is held while they type.** The moment someone picks a time, it's held for 10 minutes while they fill in their details. If someone else reaches for that same time, they're told right away. This is the part that prevents double bookings: the slot is settled on the spot, so nobody finds out later.

**Plans can change without a phone call.** Every booking comes with a private link, so a client can reschedule or cancel without asking you, and the freed-up time goes back on your page.

That's the shift: the back-and-forth doesn't get faster, it goes away.

## How it works

There are three steps, and they're the whole setup.

1. **Name your page.** Pick the preset closest to what you do: Healthcare, Spaces, Professional services, Events, or Restaurants. Services, hours, and prices arrive pre-filled, so you're editing a real page instead of staring at an empty form. Change whatever doesn't fit.
2. **Share the link.** Put it in your bio and your emails, or print it as a QR code for the door. When someone messages you to ask about times, send them the link.
3. **Switch on autopilot.** Clients book and reschedule on their own, and you stop being the person who relays the calendar.

You will create an account to publish your page, because the page and its bookings have to belong to someone. Your clients never will.

One page can also handle more than one kind of booking. Appointments, full-day bookings, and ticketed events all live on the same page, so a studio that offers private sessions and a weekend workshop doesn't need two systems. Pages work in English and Spanish, too, which matters if your clients don't all share a first language.

If you already live in Google Calendar, Google Calendar sync is available as an optional part of the Premium plan. Bookings appear on your calendar, and your clients' personal details stay in Haab. A calendar can be shared, so names, emails, and phone numbers don't belong on it.
